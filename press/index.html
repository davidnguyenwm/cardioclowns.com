Most fitness apps are about you: your goals, your streaks, your stats. Cardio Clowns is about them — the four or five people you love just enough to never, ever let win.

What it is

Create a group, share a six-character code, and every step you take becomes ammunition in a daily battle for the crown. Steps come straight from Apple Health. The leaderboard updates live and tells you exactly what it will take to pass the person ahead of you — "Almost there! +312 to pass Mom." Win the day and you wear the crown for the whole group to see, or resent.

No accounts. No friend requests. No feed full of strangers. One code gets a whole family in — kids, parents, grandparents, the college group chat — and country flags next to every name mean it works across borders.

23 seconds of it

The App Store preview: the leaderboard updating live, a rival's notification landing mid-scroll, the head-to-head tug-of-war, and the GIF picker.

The whole pitch is that a step counter can be funny, which is hard to prove in a paragraph and obvious in about ten seconds of watching it.

Why it isn't another step tracker

It solves the retention problem fitness apps actually have. Solo goal-tracking gets abandoned. Social obligation doesn't. The app's entire design bet is that you'll take the stairs to avoid losing to your brother — a mechanic no step counter has made genuinely funny before.

Push each other, built in. Long-press anyone on the leaderboard to send a jab, and the app knows the situation. Up by 1,000 steps? "Stay down there." About to get caught? "Don't choke now." Plus emoji reactions, a library of 192 reaction GIFs, and custom messages — delivered straight to your rival's wrist.

Head-to-head rivalries. Every group member is a running matchup: win–loss record, win streaks, an animated tug-of-war rope showing who's pulling ahead, biggest blowout, most painful loss. You're not chasing an abstract number — you're settling a score.

It celebrates like it means it. First place erupts in fireworks. Yesterday's winner wears the crown all day. 8,000 steps becomes a story the group brings up at dinner.

Built on Apple's stack

Cardio Clowns is what one developer can do with the full Apple stack, and it uses each piece for a reason.

SwiftUIEvery screen, on iPhone, iPad, and Apple Watch — the iPad layout adapts rather than stretches.
Apple WatchA full native app — leaderboard, history, head-to-head matchups, and messaging — not a viewer for the phone.
HealthKitSteps read directly from Apple Health. Nothing is asked for that isn't used.
CloudKitSync with no account and no server of ours. Your leaderboard is visible only to people holding your group code.
WidgetsiPhone Home Screen widget with your live standing, plus watch-face complications showing rank and steps at a glance.
App ClipA friend taps your invite link and is on the leaderboard before deciding whether to install.
StoreKit 2Free and Pro, client-side entitlements, Family Sharing on.
Swift ChartsDaily step history with finishing ranks and medals.

Few indie apps ship a Watch app, an App Clip, and widgets — and fewer do all three well.

Built for the world on day one

44 languages across 50 App Store locales, including Arabic and Hebrew with proper right-to-left layout, ten Indic languages, and both Chinese scripts. Not a retrofit — the localization architecture and an in-app language switcher were built before launch, and the app's terminology (the word for a "push," the phrase for "trash talk") was chosen deliberately per language rather than translated literally.

Screenshots and app preview videos are localized across all 50.

Accessibility

  • VoiceOver labels, values, and hints throughout, including the leaderboard, matchups, and the messaging flow.
  • Dynamic Type — every screen scales to the largest text sizes.
  • Reduce Motion honored: fireworks, the tug-of-war rope, and rank-change animations all quiet down.
  • No color-only status — wins, losses, and rank changes carry text and shape cues, not just green and red.

Privacy

No account. No ads. No third-party trackers, analytics SDKs, or advertising identifiers. Steps come from Apple Health with explicit permission and are used for the leaderboard, nothing else. Group data syncs through the user's own iCloud.

Country flags are derived from the device's time zone — the app never asks for location.

Why now

An end-of-August launch lands in the fresh-start window: back to school, the end-of-summer reset, the ramp toward New Year fitness resolutions. Cardio Clowns is the app that turns "I should get back into it" into "my group is counting on me."

What's next
